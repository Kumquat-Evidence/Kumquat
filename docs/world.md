# Kumquat world bible: Dar al-Mizan, the House of the Balance

Written 12/11/2026 with the user (answers in the 12/11 chat). Kumquat 10.0.0 builds this into the app. **Fiction:** Dar al-Mizan, its oath, the Dajij and the Astrolabe are invented. The real people, dates and works named below are real, and nothing is put in a real person's mouth as a genuine quote. The Shaykh's lines in the app are a fictional voice.

## The premise in one paragraph
Since the eleventh century a quiet guild of physicians and counters has kept one rule: *weigh before you believe*. They call themselves **Dar al-Mizan**, the House of the Balance. Their enemy is the **Dajij** (Arabic *ḍajīj*, "the din, the clamour"): the Noise. It is chance made into weather, a fog that thickens wherever samples are small and patience is short, and it has a voice that whispers the easy reading: the lucky streak, the striking subgroup, the p of 0.049. You are a new apprentice recruited in Qiddiya in 2026. Your guide is the guild's founder, **Ibn Sina**, whose voice the Astrolabe keeps.

## Places and objects
- **The Hall of the Balance** (the app itself): a lamplit reading room of parchment, ink and brass. Every screen is part of it. Today is the reading desk; Study is the library; Drill is the courtyard where apprentices spar; Toolkit is the instrument cabinet; Me is your ledger.
- **The Mizan**: a brass balance with two pans, signal and noise. It is the guild's seal and its oath.
- **The kumquat seal**: the small golden fruit stamped in orange wax on every guild document. Its skin is sweet and its flesh is sharp, the opposite of most fruit, as the truth often is. The app's icon is the seal.
- **The Astrolabe of Hamadan** (in the app, "the Kumquat Machine"): Ibn Sina's star instrument, rebuilt by later Weighers so that it measures moments rather than stars. Set it to an hour when evidence tipped the scales and you step into that hour. It only turns for a Weigher strong enough to survive the Noise of that era, which is why the jumps are gated.
- **The Archive**: a cabinet that holds every study ever run, true and forged. The daily **sealed omen** is one folio drawn from it each morning.

## History of the guild (real events in plain text, the guild's part in italics)
- **c. 1020–1037, Hamadan and Isfahan.** Ibn Sina (980–1037), called *al-Shaykh al-Raʾīs* (the chief master), finishes the *Canon of Medicine*. In its book on simple drugs he sets out conditions for testing a remedy, usually summarised as seven: the drug must be pure; it must be tried on a simple disease, not a mixture of diseases; it should be tried on contrasting cases; its strength must match the disease; the time of its action must be watched so that its effect is not confused with chance; its effect must recur in many cases; and the test must be done on humans, since a drug may act differently in a lion or a horse. *He founds Dar al-Mizan to keep those rules alive, and names their enemy the Dajij.*
- **1348–1350, Granada.** During the Black Death, Ibn al-Khatib argues from observation that the plague spreads by contagion. *The guild's counters keep the tallies in the plague years and learn that the Dajij is loudest where nobody counts.*
- **1662, London.** John Graunt analyses the Bills of Mortality: the first life tables. *A Weigher in all but name.*
- **1747, HMS Salisbury.** James Lind gives six pairs of sailors with scurvy six different treatments; the citrus pair recover. *The guild adopts the orange of the citrus as its colour.*
- **1847 to 2020.** The ten jumps of the Astrolabe (below): Semmelweis, Snow, Tuskegee, the streptomycin trial, Doll and Hill, thalidomide, Legionnaires', the HRT reversal, MERS in Riyadh, RECOVERY.
- **2026, Qiddiya.** *The guild recruits you.*

## The Shaykh (the guide)
Ibn Sina as the Astrolabe remembers him: courteous, exacting, dryly funny and impatient with sloppy thinking, never cruel. He addresses you by your rank and occasionally uses an Arabic word, always followed by its meaning. He never jokes about religion, origin or family, and he never claims you hold a real qualification.

## The Dajij (the Noise)
It works at two levels: fog and voice.
- **Fog:** a pale haze at the edge of the screen. It thickens when you have been away, when recent answers go badly, and in early-era jumps. It lifts as you answer well. It never covers a question, an answer or feedback, and it can be switched off.
- **Voice:** short whispers after mistakes, voicing the temptation that caused them ("Twelve patients is plenty"). They are shown in muted italics, briefly, away from the content.
- **Avatars:** at the end of each jump, the Dajij takes the shape of the thinking error that era fought. Beating it is the jump's duel: 6 questions, no hints, and 5 hits to win (one miss allowed).

## Rules for building it (from the world kit, applied)
- The world serves the learning: every jump is bound to a real case and topic, and no story beat blocks a review.
- Gates need real performance (85% accuracy, or mastery for the later jumps).
- Readable body text in every look. Decorative type is for headings only.
- Effects never cover the question, answers, close buttons or feedback, and the Noise has an off switch.
- Real history stays accurate. Fiction is labelled as fiction in the Archive.

## Look and sound
- **Guild interface (default):** parchment and ink by day, and a candlelit dark theme. Brass and gold rules, the signature orange as the wax-seal accent, an eight-point geometric star as the ornament, and Amiri and Cormorant headings with readable body text. Arcade, Clean and Paper stay available.
- **Era sound (generated):**
  - Hall and prologue: an oud-like pluck and a ney-like breath over a maqam-flavoured drone.
  - 1847–1900: a harpsichord-like pluck.
  - 1930s–1970s: warm radio tones.
  - 2000s on: a synth pulse.
  - The moods (Tired, Playful, Exam) keep their own styles.
