    How Kumquat teaches, and why

    Natural frequencies. Bayesian problems taught as “of 1000 people…” are solved by ~75–80% of medical students, versus ~20% with formulas. That’s the Tree drill, and it’s why p values are taught the same way.

    Predict, then learn. Lessons open with a myth check: you commit to an answer before the explanation, which makes the correction stick.

    Retrieval and spacing. You answer before you see; misses return after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. Confident misses come back first.

    Interleaving. Mixed drills make you pick the method from the problem, which is the exam skill.

    Generation. For calculations you type the number first; options appear only if you ask.

    Faded worked examples, teach-back, calibration. Scaffolding fades as you grow; you explain ideas in writing and compare with a model; you flag guesses.

    Hold anything (a number, a chip, a card) to get an explanation or more actions.

    Math foundations

    Math gym

    The three bits of maths that epidemiology leans on most: where a value sits on the normal curve, how odds relate to probability, and what skew does to averages.

    1 · The normal curve · SBP mean 120, SD 10

    Bands show ±1, ±2, ±3 SD: 68%, 95%, 99.7%. Tails beyond +1, +2, +3 SD: 16%, 2.5%, 0.15%.

    2 · Probability ↔ odds
    odds = p / (1 − p)
    p = odds / (1 + odds)

    Small probabilities: odds ≈ probability. That’s why OR ≈ RR for rare outcomes.

    CI dance

    True mean systolic BP in a workforce is 130 mmHg (SD 15). Each line is one study that samples n workers and builds a confidence interval. Count how often the truth gets caught.

    Confidence level

    “95% confidence” describes the method: across many studies, 95% of intervals contain the true value. Any single interval either does or doesn’t.

    Bigger n → narrower intervals. Higher confidence → wider intervals.

    Hierarchy of evidence (for treatment questions)
    The 3-second decoder

    Did the investigator assign the exposure? Yes → experimental (randomised? → RCT).

    No, observational. Start from exposure and follow forward → cohort (RR). Start from outcome and look back → case-control (OR). One snapshot → cross-sectional (prevalence). Groups, not people → ecological.

    Choosing tests

    Test picker

    Two or three questions about your data and you have the test. Then practise until you skip the tree.

    The cheat line

    Normal & 2 groups → t-test (paired or independent). Not normal → Mann–Whitney or Wilcoxon. 3+ groups → ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis. Categories → χ² or Fisher; paired → McNemar. Time to event → log-rank, Cox.

    Measuring disease

    Outbreak simulator

    A town of 10,000. Ten people arrive infected. Set how contagious the pathogen is and how many people are already immune, and watch the epidemic run.

    R₀ = contacts × chance of transmission × duration. Shorten any one (distancing, masks, isolation) and the curve flattens.

    Herd immunity threshold = 1 − 1/R₀.
